## What it is and does

A presentation sheet showing four variants of a minimalist fullscreen portfolio-theme hero. Each composition pairs a double-exposure portrait with a seasonal title—Summer, Winter, Spring, or Autumn—plus global navigation, brief placeholder copy, a portfolio call to action, and left/right slider controls.

## Aesthetic family

Airy editorial minimalism; fine-art portfolio; double-exposure photography; monochrome fashion-template design.

## Composition and hierarchy

Every variant uses the same quiet frame: a tiny boxed `B` mark at upper left, a thin horizontal menu at upper right, and chevrons centered on the outer edges. The portrait occupies the left half and crosses the centerline softly, while the seasonal title anchors the right half. A script eyebrow, short rule motif, two lines of body copy, and a small button form a compact vertical stack beneath the title. Repetition makes the image treatment—not layout novelty—the changing focal point.

## Typography

The main seasonal words use a clean, widely tracked geometric sans serif in uppercase. Navigation and buttons use even smaller uppercase sans serif text with generous letter spacing. A restrained handwritten script adds one delicate contrast above each title. The hierarchy depends on scale, spacing, and case rather than heavy weight.

## Color, texture, and imagery

White dominates, with black type and extremely light gray controls. The double exposures introduce washed teal water, sand, forest green, mountain gray, and warm sunset tones. Texture comes from hair, foliage, water, and cloud forms feathered into the portraits; the interface itself remains almost completely flat and untextured.

## Interaction and motion

The visible side chevrons and repeated seasonal frames clearly suggest a fullscreen slider. No motion is present in the static evidence. A matching implementation could crossfade the portrait masks and landscape layers while the title and caption shift subtly, with keyboard and swipe navigation, but those details remain design recommendations rather than observed behavior.

## What to borrow

Borrow the stable split layout that lets one expressive image technique carry an entire series. Use a single oversized word, a small script eyebrow, minimal supporting copy, and generous empty space so the double exposure remains legible. Keep controls consistently peripheral and quiet.

## Do not copy

Do not reuse the Borderland mark, exact seasonal sequence, source portraits, landscape photographs, menu labels, title arrangement, or portfolio button styling. Do not rely on low-contrast controls that disappear for keyboard or low-vision users; preserve the delicacy while meeting accessible contrast and focus requirements.

## Copy-ready design brief

Create a quiet fullscreen portfolio hero system built around original double-exposure portrait photography. Hold the interface constant across several thematic slides: a tiny boxed monogram in the upper-left, compact uppercase navigation in the upper-right, accessible previous/next controls at the sides, and a balanced two-column center. Let a feathered portrait-and-landscape composite fill the left side and place one widely tracked uppercase theme word on the right. Add a short handwritten eyebrow, a fine rule, two restrained lines of copy, and one understated outlined action. Use bright white space, near-black typography, and muted photographic color only inside the portrait. Transition slides with slow mask crossfades and subtle text movement while supporting keyboard, swipe, reduced-motion, and visible focus states. Match the source's airy double-exposure mood without borrowing its brand, seasonal labels, photography, or exact layout.

## What it is and does

A space-education landing-page concept branded “spaceedu.” Its hero introduces Earth as “The Blue Planet,” provides a short educational description, and directs visitors toward learning more or playing media. A compact top navigation offers Planets, Trailer, Tickets, Blog, and an Enroll action.

## Aesthetic family

Premium cinematic science editorial with planetarium drama, museum-style information design, and a minimal dark SaaS navigation layer.

## Composition and hierarchy

The navigation occupies a thin band across the top with the logo at far left, links centered-right, and a bright Enroll pill at the edge. The hero copy forms a compact column in the lower-left quadrant, while the Earth consumes most of the right side and expands beyond the viewport during the animation. The scale contrast makes the planet the emotional anchor; the title, paragraph, and paired actions create a clear secondary reading path.

## Typography

A large classical serif gives “EARTH” an authoritative editorial tone. It is paired with a clean geometric sans serif for the logo, navigation, eyebrow, body copy, and buttons. Uppercase letterspacing distinguishes labels from prose, while the compact paragraph width keeps the educational copy readable against the dark field.

## Color, texture, and imagery

The palette is nearly monochrome: midnight blue, black, icy white, and cyan. Photoreal cloud and terrain detail supplies most of the texture, with a bright blue atmospheric edge separating the globe from space. Fine stars and soft bloom add depth; there are no competing decorative colors.

## Interaction and motion

The public video shows the Earth scaling and drifting until it becomes an immersive, near-full-frame surface behind the fixed top navigation. The active navigation item receives a cyan underline. The paired hero controls imply a primary learn-more action and a circular media/play action, but click outcomes and hover behavior are not evidenced.

## What to borrow

Borrow the single-object hero strategy: one exceptionally large, high-quality subject; a small editorial copy block; and navigation that stays visually quiet. Use serif/sans contrast to separate storytelling from utility. Reserve cyan for atmospheric light, active state, and focus cues, and use scale motion to turn a familiar object into an immersive environment.

## Do not copy

Do not reuse the spaceedu identity, “The Blue Planet / EARTH” copy, exact planet asset, navigation labels, button arrangement, or the same globe trajectory. Avoid using scientific imagery merely as decoration when the product has no meaningful connection to it.

## Copy-ready design brief

Create a cinematic educational landing page centered on one monumental natural subject. Set the experience on a midnight-blue field with sparse atmospheric texture, then let a photoreal hero object dominate roughly two-thirds of the canvas and extend beyond the viewport. Place a compact story block in the lower-left area: a wide-tracked uppercase eyebrow, an oversized elegant serif title, two to four lines of restrained sans-serif copy, and two simple actions. Keep the top navigation extremely thin, using small white sans-serif labels, one cyan active indicator, and a bright pill-shaped conversion button. Animate the hero object with a slow scale-and-drift move that increases immersion while the interface remains stable. Limit the palette to deep navy, white, and one cool luminous accent.

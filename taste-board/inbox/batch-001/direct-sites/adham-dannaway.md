## What it is and does

A personal portfolio for a hybrid product designer and front-end developer. The identity is communicated immediately through a literal designer/coder split portrait: expressive illustration on one side, photography and faint code on the other.

## Composition and hierarchy

The evidence image uses a wide, centered hero with the portrait forming the vertical axis. Large role labels sit on opposing sides—“designer” left and `<coder>` right—making the concept legible before any supporting copy. Generous negative space keeps the single visual idea dominant.

## Typography

Heavy geometric sans serif, lowercase, oversized, and nearly black. The coder label borrows angle brackets as a compact technical cue. Faint monospaced code behind the photographic half provides a secondary typographic texture without competing with the headline.

## Color, texture, and imagery

Predominantly white, black, and warm photographic skin tones. The designer half introduces restrained cyan, coral, mustard, blush, and black brushwork. The contrast between tactile paint and subdued code is the key image-making device.

Borrow the speed of comprehension: one strong visual metaphor, one central axis, two opposing role labels, and enough whitespace that the concept reads instantly. Use texture differences—not extra explanatory copy—to distinguish complementary capabilities.

## Copy-ready design brief

Create a bright, minimal portfolio hero built around one instantly readable dual-identity metaphor. Center a custom subject image on a strong vertical divide, contrasting an expressive tactile treatment on one side with a precise technical treatment on the other. Place one oversized, lowercase role label on each side in a bold geometric sans serif; let a tiny syntax cue signal the technical role. Keep the canvas mostly white, reserve color for a small pastel accent palette, and use faint specialist texture behind the technical half. The result should feel clever, personal, confident, and immediately legible—without reusing the source portrait, wording, or exact split-face execution.

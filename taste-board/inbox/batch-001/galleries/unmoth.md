## What it is and does

Unmoth is a branding studio that turns ideas into launch-ready brand systems through a fixed sprint. The page presents a concise promise, a grid of selected identity work, a service checklist, and a three-stage process from kickoff through delivery.

## Aesthetic family

Dark neo-brutalist studio portfolio; precision-grid minimalism; technical editorial identity.

## Visual vocabulary

- Near-black monochrome canvas
- Hairline modular grid
- Geometric wireframe diagrams
- Oversized editorial serif statement
- Condensed technical labels
- Numbered system notation
- Three-column project matrix
- Electric-green status dot

## Composition and hierarchy

The page starts with a highly structured hero: a fixed-width header, an enormous gridded diagram, and a centered two-line promise below it. Subsequent sections retain the same hairline grid and small numerical labels. Work appears as a compact three-column matrix; services split a large statement from a checklist; process steps become three equal diagram cards. Large statements provide rare moments of scale inside an otherwise restrained technical system.

## Typography

A contemporary sans serif handles explanatory statements and project copy, while a narrow monospaced or technical display face labels controls, indices, and micro-navigation. A high-contrast serif enters only for the largest emotional promise, creating a deliberate collision between brand warmth and system precision.

## Color, texture, and imagery

Black and charcoal dominate, with white text, dim gray rules, and one tiny acid-green status accent. Texture comes from the dense grid, fine vector lines, small registration points, and diverse project thumbnails rather than surface effects. The work grid introduces muted cream, forest green, coral, and product imagery without disturbing the monochrome shell.

## What to borrow

Borrow the coherent grid that governs hero art, portfolio thumbnails, service lists, and process diagrams. Use one tiny live-status color as a functional accent, and reserve expressive serif type for a single emotional message so the technical system never becomes sterile.

## Do not copy

Do not reproduce the Unmoth name, 14-day sprint claim, exact radial hero diagram, green status dot placement, project thumbnails, service wording, or three process icons. Avoid treating arbitrary wireframes as decoration; every line should reinforce a real layout or process concept.

## Copy-ready design brief

Create a dark studio portfolio built on a visible hairline grid that behaves like an operating system for the whole page. Open with a sparse header, one large geometric data-like diagram, and a centered two-line promise set in an expressive serif. Use condensed technical labels, tiny indices, and a single bright status accent around the edges. Continue into a compact three-column work matrix, then split a strong service statement from a precise checklist and finish with three equal process cards containing original wireframe diagrams. Keep the palette near-black, white, and graphite; let selected work introduce muted color. Motion should follow scroll progression and subtle system-state changes, matching the source's rigorous, slightly mysterious feel without borrowing its name, sprint claim, diagram, projects, or copy.
